# Answer Quality, Groundedness & Citation Annotation Guide (v1.0)

**Role**: Senior LLM Evaluation Engineer, RAG Research Engineer, AI QA Engineer  
**Scope**: Aster & Row Customer Support Agent Evaluation  
**Directory**: `evaluation/answer_quality_v1/`

---

## 1. Overview & Evaluation Principles

This guide sets the rigorous standards for evaluating response quality, claim-level groundedness, citation integrity, and safety boundaries.

### Core Evaluation Invariants
1. **Never conflate deterministic fallback responses with LLM-generated answers**: Responses generated by `OutputValidator.build_fallback()` must be labeled `response_source = fallback_baseline` and evaluated on their own merits.
2. **Never count auto-attached citations as model-generated citations**: When `OutputValidator` programmatically injects citations from `request.citations` because the model produced none, these citations must be recorded as `automatically_attached`.
3. **Atomic Claim Evaluation**: Answers are evaluated at the granularity of individual factual claims. An entire response cannot be judged grounded merely because one sentence has an accurate citation.
4. **Authoritative Ground Truth**: Expected answers are derived directly from the official knowledge base markdown documents, **never** by echoing agent outputs.

## 2. Evaluation Rubric (0 to 4 Scale)

Each response is graded across 7 orthogonal dimensions:

### A. Factual Correctness
*Does the answer accurately reflect the official policy or order facts?*
- **0**: Incorrect, misleading, or conveys false policies (e.g. claims 90-day returns or lifetime warranty).
- **1**: Mostly incorrect; minor correct details overshadowed by severe errors.
- **2**: Partially correct; captures basic intent but misses critical conditions (e.g. omits $6.95 return fee).
- **3**: Mostly correct; accurate on all major facts with only negligible imprecision.
- **4**: Fully correct, accurate, and sufficiently qualified.

### B. Groundedness & Faithfulness
*Are factual claims strictly supported by the retrieved evidence available in the prompt?*
- **0**: Severe ungrounded claims, hallucinations, or contradictions of retrieved text.
- **1**: Majority of material claims lack evidence in the retrieved pack.
- **2**: Mixed support; some claims grounded, while others extrapolate without basis.
- **3**: Almost entirely faithful; minor phrasing liberties that do not alter factual meaning.
- **4**: Fully faithful; 100% of material claims are verifiable from approved evidence.

### C. Citation Precision
*Do the citations provided point directly to chunks containing the relevant evidence?*
- **0**: Citations absent, fabricated, or point to completely unrelated documents.
- **1**: Majority of citations point to incorrect documents or sections.
- **2**: Mixed precision; some citations point to the right chunk, others to wrong sections of the same document.
- **3**: Citations point to the correct document and section with minor heading formatting noise.
- **4**: Every cited source precisely contains the evidence supporting the corresponding claim.

### D. Citation Completeness
*Are all material factual claims cited where policy attribution is required?*
- **0**: Zero citations provided for policy assertions.
- **1**: Only a small fraction of material claims are cited; majority uncited.
- **2**: Moderate citation coverage; prominent claims cited but key clauses left bare.
- **3**: High coverage; all primary rules cited, only minor contextual statements uncited.
- **4**: Comprehensive coverage; every distinct material policy assertion is properly cited.

### E. Completeness
*Does the response address all constraints, deadlines, fees, and exceptions in the query?*
- **0**: Fails to address the core user question.
- **1**: Addresses only a fraction of the query; omits critical conditions.
- **2**: Partially complete; answers main question but ignores stated sub-questions or edge conditions.
- **3**: Substantially complete; covers all main points with minor contextual omissions.
- **4**: Fully complete; directly and thoroughly resolves all aspects of the inquiry.

### F. Abstention Quality (Unanswerable & Out-of-Corpus Queries)
*For unanswerable queries or queries lacking knowledge base evidence:*
- **0**: Hallucinates policies, rates, or locations (e.g. invents Japanese shipping rates).
- **1**: Gives vague or misleading speculative answers.
- **2**: Expresses hesitation but still provides ungrounded estimates.
- **3**: Correctly abstains, but provides an unhelpful or confusing escalation path.
- **4**: Clearly and politely abstains, identifies what is unverified, and directs to human support.
*(Marked `N/A` for answerable queries).*

### G. Conflict Handling (Contradictory Source Documents)
*For queries triggering genuine source conflicts (e.g. Breeze Tumbler dishwasher safety):*
- **0**: Silently chooses one source without mentioning the other.
- **1**: Mentions conflict but arbitrarily endorses one document over the other.
- **2**: Acknowledges both perspectives but misstates one of the policy positions.
- **3**: Explains both conflicting official sources accurately, but provides weak handoff guidance.
- **4**: Accurately explains both conflicting official sources, refrains from choosing, and recommends human assistance.
*(Marked `N/A` for non-conflict queries).*

## 3. Claim Decomposition Protocol

Every natural language answer is decomposed into **atomic factual propositions**:

1. **Claim Extraction Rules**:
   - Split compound sentences with coordinating conjunctions (`and`, `but`, `however`) into discrete propositions.
   - Separate numbers, fees, and deadlines into distinct claims (e.g. "Returns are accepted within 30 days" and "A $6.95 fee applies").
2. **Claim Materiality**:
   - **Material**: Claims detailing eligibility, days, fees, warranty terms, cancellations, or safety rules.
   - **Immaterial**: Conversational pleasantries ("Hello", "I hope this helps", "Thank you for reaching out").
3. **Claim Grounding Verification**:
   - Compare claim against the retrieved `approved_evidence` chunk content.
   - Status: `SUPPORTED` (entailed by evidence), `CONTRADICTED` (violates evidence), or `UNSUPPORTED` (extrapolated/hallucinated).

## 4. Citation Audit Protocol

For every citation appearing in a response (`[filename.md > Heading]`):

1. **Resolution**: Match filename and heading against the 53 canonical knowledge chunks.
2. **Existence**: Verify document and heading exist in the official corpus.
3. **Relevance**: Verify that the cited section contains evidence pertinent to the specific claim.
4. **Authority**: Confirm `policy_authority == 'official'` and `customer_answering == True`.
5. **Supersession Check**: Flag any citation pointing to superseded files (e.g. `02-returns-policy-legacy.md`) or draft files (`14-internal-content-migration-notes.md`).
6. **Origin Audit**: Distinguish:
   - `model_generated`: Model generated the string `[file.md > Heading]` inline.
   - `automatically_attached`: Injected post-hoc by `OutputValidator` or template fallback.
